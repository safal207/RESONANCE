Who Saw the Outcome?

Authorization is only the first half of agent trust. After an AI system acts, a harder question begins: who observed what actually happened, from where, and against which evidence?

By RESONANCE Editorial

Issue 001 · The Age of Agents

Core claim

The consequence needs a witness. And the witness needs provenance.

Invariant

decision_provenance != outcome_provenance

Missing axis

observer + vantage Source class alone cannot tell us who saw the outcome or from where.

AI-agent safety is usually framed around a pre-action question: was the agent allowed to do this? That matters. It is not enough. Once an agent touches money, code, infrastructure, permissions or another consequential system, a second question becomes equally important: who says what actually happened?

A valid authorization can prove that an action was permitted. It cannot, by itself, prove that the action happened, happened once, settled correctly, or was independently observed.

The authorization trap

Imagine an agent asks to transfer funds. A guardrail evaluates the proposal and returns ALLOW. The transfer API is called. A response arrives. The agent reports success.

proposed action→decision→ALLOW→execution→?

The question mark is where many trust architectures become vague. The pre-action decision and the post-action outcome are different facts, potentially produced by different parties, from different observation positions, with different evidence.

Decision provenance is not outcome provenance

The boundary is simple enough to write as an invariant:

decision_provenance != outcome_provenance

A decision issuer could be an in-process policy engine, an external guardrail service, a human approver or an independent verifier. The later outcome could be observed by the agent itself, the tool provider, an internal ledger, a settlement network, a blockchain RPC endpoint or an external auditor.

Those roles should not collapse into one trust domain by convenience. Otherwise a system can silently become both actor and grader.

Four questions

1

Was the action authorized?
Which policy and authority produced the decision?

2

Is this the same operation?
Bind the later claim to the same logical operation and concrete execution.

3

What outcome was observed?
Settled, failed, uncertain, rolled back, duplicated or something else?

4

Who observed it?
From what vantage, and against which evidence?

`source_class` is necessary — and insufficient

A public architecture discussion around CrewAI's proposed GuardrailProvider produced an unusually useful comparison. A RESONANCE causal-provenance proposal was checked against an independently developed, already-shipped verdict_outcome mechanism.

The comparison found that the shipped mechanism had independently arrived at several of the same boundaries: outcome-side source class was separate from decision-side source class, the outcome cited the earlier decision, and the evidence mechanism travelled with the outcome observation.

Then the comparison exposed a concrete gap: there was no distinct outcome_observer_id or outcome_vantage beyond source_class.

Source class tells us how the outcome was established. Observer identity and vantage tell us who observed it, and from where.

Consider two records:

source_class: attested
outcome_observer_id: verifier-A
outcome_vantage: ethereum_rpc
source_class: attested
outcome_observer_id: wallet-provider
outcome_vantage: internal_ledger

Both can be honestly labelled attested. They are not equivalent observations. One may see public settlement state; the other may see internal accounting state. Their blind spots and independence properties differ.

A minimal outcome-provenance receipt

The answer is not necessarily a giant schema. A deliberately small provider-neutral spine can preserve the identities that matter:

logical_operation_id: op-123
execution_id: attempt-2
pre_action_decision_ref: sha256:...
observed_outcome: settled
outcome_provenance:
  source_class: attested
  observer_id: verifier-A
  vantage: ethereum_rpc
  observation_basis: on-chain-settlement
  evidence_ref: sha256:...
observed_at: 2026-08-13T00:00:00Z

The exact field names can change. The causal structure should not.

intent→decision→execution→outcome→observer→vantage→evidence

Why independent convergence matters

The interesting part was not that two people liked the same architecture. The external comparison was made against a shipped mechanism, named concrete field-level overlap, and exposed one missing dimension.

causal proposal→shipped implementation comparison→field convergence→observer/vantage gap

That makes the sequence falsifiable. Another reader can inspect the public comments and challenge the mapping.

The claim boundary is equally important: this is not CrewAI adoption, endorsement or certification. It is an externally observed architecture convergence that generated a concrete next test.

Do not patch the schema just because the gap is interesting

The external implementer explicitly chose not to perform another reactive schema edit after several real bug-fix rounds. That restraint is part of the signal.

Before observer and vantage become a standard field pair, they should survive mutation and conformance tests that distinguish:

  • same source class, different observer;
  • same observer, different vantage;
  • missing observer identity;
  • missing or ambiguous vantage;
  • an evidence mechanism incompatible with the claimed vantage;
  • two observers reporting conflicting outcomes;
  • self-observation incorrectly promoted to independent observation.

If changing the observer or vantage never changes a verification outcome, the new fields may be decoration. If intentionally wrong provenance changes the verdict, the axis is load-bearing.

The money case makes the problem obvious

Consider a programmable wallet with a spending policy. The policy service says the transaction was allowed. The wallet provider says the debit succeeded. A chain observer says settlement reverted. A webhook says completed. The accounting ledger remains unchanged.

There is no useful universal label called verified=true for that situation.

A verifier now needs

A

Authorization identity
Which action and policy were approved?

B

Execution identity
Which attempt produced each observation?

C

Observation identity
Who produced each outcome claim?

D

Observation vantage
API, ledger, chain, webhook, audit log or another plane?

E

Evidence
What can another party inspect or recompute?

F

Cross-plane invariants
Which states must agree before the action is considered settled?

This is where outcome provenance stops being metadata and becomes financial control infrastructure.

And it is not only about payments

Code agents: did the patch merge, or did the agent only observe a local branch?

Infrastructure agents: did the deployment reach the target environment, or only the orchestrator's desired state?

Data agents: did the write commit to the source of record, or only to a replica or cache?

Security agents: was a credential revoked at the authority, or only marked revoked in a local control plane?

Scientific agents: was a result independently reproduced, or merely re-reported from the originating pipeline?

The consequence needs a witness. The witness needs provenance.

A new layer in the agent economy

As agents gain permission to create durable consequences, a market is likely to grow around independent observation of those consequences. The valuable layer may not be another agent framework. It may be infrastructure that can answer across heterogeneous systems:

What was intended?
What was authorized?
What executed?
What changed?
Who observed it?
From where?
Against which evidence?
Can another party recompute the claim?

That layer is relevant to agent payments, programmable wallets, deployment systems, governance, compliance and any workflow where software can cause an irreversible or expensive state transition.

Hot question

When your AI agent says a consequential action succeeded, who is allowed to declare that outcome?

And can a third party verify that observer's identity, vantage and evidence without trusting the agent itself?

Inspect the external comparison → Open Engineering Signal 008 →

What builders can do now

Take one consequential workflow and draw two records instead of one:

PRE-ACTION
intent → policy → decision → decision provenance

POST-ACTION
execution → observed outcome → observer → vantage → evidence

Then try to join them using only stable references that a third party could inspect. Where the join breaks, you have found a real trust boundary.

Authorization asks whether the action was permitted. Outcome provenance asks what the world became — and who can prove it.
