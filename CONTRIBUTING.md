# Contributing to RESONANCE

RESONANCE welcomes contributors who can improve understanding, evidence, or verification.

## What we want

Strong contributions usually do at least one of these:

- uncover a primary source or dataset;
- explain a technical or scientific mechanism clearly;
- test a widely repeated claim;
- reconstruct a timeline;
- identify an important counterexample;
- reproduce a benchmark or calculation;
- map an emerging market or technology stack;
- interview a relevant practitioner, researcher, builder, or affected person;
- improve the evidence package behind an existing story.

## What we do not want

- generic AI-generated summaries;
- unattributed aggregation;
- promotional copy disguised as analysis;
- fabricated citations, quotes, data, or interviews;
- confident claims without visible support;
- plagiarism or lightly rewritten source material.

## Proposal format

Open an issue or submit a pull request with:

1. **Working title**
2. **One-sentence thesis**
3. **Why now**
4. **Material claims to verify**
5. **Likely primary sources**
6. **Known uncertainty or disagreement**
7. **Why RESONANCE readers should care**

## Writing workflow

Use [`content/ARTICLE_TEMPLATE.md`](content/ARTICLE_TEMPLATE.md) for major pieces.

Recommended lifecycle:

```text
proposal
  ↓
research
  ↓
draft
  ↓
source verification
  ↓
causality / uncertainty review
  ↓
editorial review
  ↓
publish
  ↓
corrections / updates
```

## AI-assisted work

AI tools are allowed and encouraged when they improve research or production efficiency.

But contributors remain responsible for the final work.

AI-generated factual statements, citations, quotations, calculations, summaries, or interpretations must be verified against underlying evidence before publication.

Never submit invented sources or synthetic interviews as reporting.

## Attribution

Credit original researchers, reporters, developers, data providers, and contributors clearly.

If an idea comes from another person, say so.

If analysis reproduces another person's method, cite it.

If a contributor materially improves an evidence package, preserve that contribution in the record.

## Corrections

If you discover a material error in published work, open an issue or pull request describing:

- the claim that is wrong;
- the supporting evidence for the correction;
- the proposed replacement;
- whether the correction changes the article's conclusion.

## Standard

The goal is not to sound certain.

The goal is to be **inspectably trustworthy**.
