# RESONANCE Science

**Evidence before conclusion. Translation before action.**

RESONANCE Science is the journal's evidence-first desk for biology, AI for Science, computational research, reproducibility and scientific decision-making.

The desk does not exist to summarize papers faster. It exists to make the path from **observation to conclusion to real-world decision** inspectable.

## Core question

A scientific result can be valid inside its experimental scope and still be insufficient for a broader decision.

RESONANCE Science therefore asks:

> What is known, what is inferred, what remains uncertain, and what additional evidence would materially change the decision?

## Editorial chain

```text
OBSERVATION
  ↓
SOURCE / DATA
  ↓
EVIDENCE STATE
  ↓
INTERPRETATION
  ↓
CAUSAL BOUNDARY
  ↓
TRANSLATION GAP
  ↓
DECISION READINESS
  ↓
NEXT DISCRIMINATING EVIDENCE
```

This is intentionally stricter than a conventional paper summary.

## What every Science report should preserve

At minimum:

- **Claim** — the narrow statement being evaluated;
- **Source** — paper, dataset, supplement, repository or other primary material;
- **Evidence state** — reported, recalculated, independently replicated, associative, causal, unresolved, contradicted or not applicable;
- **Scope** — population, system, environment, timeframe and experimental context in which the evidence applies;
- **Uncertainty** — what is not yet established;
- **Negative or conflicting evidence** — observations that weaken or bound the interpretation;
- **Translation limit** — what prevents movement from the current evidence state to a broader biological or operational decision;
- **Next discriminating evidence** — the smallest additional observation, analysis or validation that would most change confidence;
- **Decision boundary** — what should not yet be claimed or authorized.

## The translation-readiness problem

Many scientific workflows implicitly compress this path:

```text
paper → conclusion → action
```

RESONANCE Science expands it:

```text
paper
  ↓
measured result
  ↓
interpretation
  ↓
causal confidence
  ↓
transfer assumptions
  ↓
external validity
  ↓
decision readiness
```

The missing links are often where the most important scientific risk lives.

## Publication formats

### Science Signal
A compact evidence-first verification of a fresh scientific claim or headline. Science Signal 001 (X(2370)) is the first live example already published in the journal.

### Science Report
A structured evidence map around one scientific claim or decision boundary.

### Field Translation
A focused analysis of what would be required to move from controlled evidence to a real-world setting.

### Research Conversation
A short publication built around a scientist's correction, unresolved question or methodological pain point. Quotes are used only with permission; otherwise insights are generalized.

First live example:

- [`Research Conversation 001 — When a metadata blocker becomes a context-dependent scientific rule`](../reports/science/conversations/001-live-seq-replicate-semantics.md)

### Verified Research Artifact
A report paired with reproducible code, source manifests, deterministic checks or evidence bundles.

## Relationship to TRACE / Kairos Gate

RESONANCE is the publication layer.

TRACE / Kairos Gate can supply structured evidence-state and claim-lineage artifacts where appropriate.

The intended loop is:

```text
scientific question
  ↓
source evidence
  ↓
structured uncertainty
  ↓
researcher feedback
  ↓
revised evidence map
  ↓
reproducible artifact
  ↓
RESONANCE publication
```

A publication is not itself proof. The underlying evidence and uncertainty must remain inspectable.

## Research-only boundary

RESONANCE Science does **not** provide:

- wet-lab protocols or optimization instructions;
- guide or construct design;
- clinical advice or therapeutic recommendations;
- biological safety approval;
- experiment authorization;
- operational biosecurity-sensitive procedures.

The desk is for evidence interpretation, provenance, reproducibility, uncertainty and decision boundaries.

## Editorial principles

1. **Do not upgrade association into causality.**
2. **Do not treat a maximum as a typical result.**
3. **Do not erase negative, null or conflicting evidence.**
4. **Do not infer biological replicates from technical identifiers without evidence.**
5. **Do not confuse publication-quality evidence with field-decision readiness.**
6. **Prefer a precise unresolved state over a confident fiction.**
7. **A scientist's correction changes the evidence map; it does not disappear into private correspondence.**
8. **No external researcher or institution is presented as endorsing RESONANCE unless they explicitly do so.**
